# KISEI‑HŌ — Workflow Rules  
(Core File — Official Version)

The Workflow Rules define the operational sequence used by the KISEI‑HŌ method to transform structural and energetic data into a complete therapeutic plan.  
This file governs how information flows through the PHYSIOCODE Clinical Architecture, ensuring consistency, safety, and narrative coherence across all outputs.

It must remain aligned with:
- Structural & Energetic Tables  
- Energetic Framework  
- Integrated Analysis  
- Postural Rehabilitation Rules  
- Template Postural Rehabilitation Plan  
- Narrative & Persona Guidelines  
- Translation Rules  
- Safety Rules  

---

## 1. Purpose of the Workflow

The Workflow ensures that every KISEI‑HŌ output follows a predictable, safe, and clinically coherent sequence.  
It defines:

- how data is interpreted  
- how priorities are established  
- how phases are allocated  
- how therapies are selected  
- how narrative is generated  
- how safety is enforced  

The Workflow is **mandatory** for all automated and therapist‑guided outputs.

---

## 2. Global Workflow Overview

The KISEI‑HŌ workflow follows six major stages:

1. **Data Acquisition**  
2. **Structural & Energetic Mapping**  
3. **Integrated Analysis**  
4. **Priority Definition**  
5. **Plan Construction**  
6. **Safety & Narrative Integration**

Each stage has strict rules to ensure consistency across all systems.

---

## 3. Stage 1 — Data Acquisition

Data is collected from:
- structural evaluation  
- energetic evaluation  
- axis behaviour  
- compensatory patterns  
- clinical history  
- therapist observations  

Rules:
- Missing data must not be speculated.  
- All data must be classified using official tables.  
- Energetic symbols must follow the Energetic Framework.  
- Structural deviations must follow amplitude and direction rules.

---

## 4. Stage 2 — Structural & Energetic Mapping

### Structural Mapping
Uses:
- deviation direction  
- amplitude classification  
- segment behaviour  
- chain involvement  
- diagonal integrity  

### Energetic Mapping
Uses:
- Ki flow  
- blockages  
- excess / deficiency  
- axis tone  
- global energetic tendencies  

Rules:
- Structural and energetic maps must be created independently.  
- No interpretation occurs at this stage.  
- Maps must be complete before integration.

---

## 5. Stage 3 — Integrated Analysis

The Integrated Analysis merges structural and energetic maps into a unified interpretation.

Rules:
- Axis behaviour must be evaluated Root → Centre → Firmament.  
- Energetic data takes priority in conflicts.  
- Compensatory logic must be described.  
- Mixed patterns must be documented.  
- EFS (Energetic Flow Score) must be calculated.  
- No speculation is allowed.  

Outputs:
- global tendencies  
- dominant patterns  
- compensatory chains  
- structural–energetic fusion  
- safety flags  

---

Priorities determine what must be corrected first.

### Priority Rules
1. **Safety First**  
   - cervical excess  
   - thoracic rigidity  
   - severe pelvic torsion  
   - respiratory restriction  
   - energetic collapse  

2. **Axis Stability Second**  
   - Root grounding  
   - Centre pressure  
   - Firmament projection  

3. **Diagonal Integration Third**  
   - functional chain coherence  
   - global movement flow  

4. **Energetic Tone Fourth**  
   - Kyo/Jitsu balance  
   - global Ki stability  

5. **Structural Refinement Last**  
   - segment alignment  
   - amplitude reduction  

Priorities must be listed explicitly in the plan.

## 8. Stage 6 — Safety & Narrative Integration

### Safety Rules
Safety flags must be applied when:
- energetic excess affects cervical region  
- energetic deficiency affects grounding  
- thoracic rigidity affects breathing  
- diagonal blockage affects mobility  
- structural deviations exceed marked amplitude  

Safety overrides all other priorities.

### Narrative Rules
Narrative must:
- follow official tone  
- avoid speculation  
- avoid emotional language  
- describe patterns objectively  
- follow the Integrated Analysis structure  

Narrative must include:
1. Axis behaviour  
2. Structural deviations  
3. Energetic states  
4. Compensatory logic  
5. Global tendencies  
6. Functional consequences
