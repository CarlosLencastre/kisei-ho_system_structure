## 5. Body Tension Lines — BTL (v1.1 NEW)
**BTL** are physical pathways of tension across the body, detectable through manual assessment, that reflect structural and energetic patterns.

### BTL Primary Pathways
| BTL | Pathway | Structural Correlation | Energetic Correlation | Kyo/Jitsu Indicators ||-----|---------|----------------------|----------------------|---------------------|| **Anterior BTL** | Jaw → Chest → Hip flexors | Chest expansion limited, forward head | Energy held in upper body | Jitsu in upper, may be Kyo in lower || **Posterior BTL** | Skull → Spine → Heels | Back tension, spinal rigidity | Energy movement along spine | Jitsu in upper back, may be Kyo in lower || **Lateral BTL** | Ear → Side body → Foot | Side bending, leg length discrepancy | Energy asymmetry | Unilateral Jitsu or Kyo || **Spiral BTL** | Diagonal crossing pattern | Rotation limited, scoliosis tendency | Energy disrupted diagonally | Mixed patterns |

### BTL Tension Classification
| Level | Description | Structural Indicator | Energetic Indicator ||-------|-------------|---------------------|---------------------|| **Mild** | Slight tension along pathway | Minor restriction | Minor Flow disruption || **Moderate** | Noticeable tension with tissue changes | Moderate restriction | Stagnant or Rebellious Flow || **Marked** | Significant tension with clear patterns | Marked restriction | Excess Flow with Jitsu pattern |

### BTL-Official Integration
When BTL tension is identified:1. Classify BTL type (Anterior, Posterior, Lateral, Spiral)2. Assess tension level (Mild, Moderate, Marked)3. Correlate with Flow State (Smooth, Stagnant, Rebellious, Deficient, Excess)4. Identify Kyo/Jitsu pattern at BTL location5. Map BTL junctions (where BTL crosses joints or other BTLs)6. Prepare for Pattern Integration and Narrative Transformation

## 9. Edge Case Validation

### Handling Conflicting DataIf structural and energetic data conflict (e.g., structural data suggests upper-dominant organisation but energetic data suggests lower-dominant):
1. **Prioritise energetic data** — energetic states reflect the body's current adaptive response2. **Cross-reference BTL** — check which BTL pathway correlates with each interpretation3. **Cross-reference Flow States** — Flow States provide quality beyond simple state4. **Consult Kyo/Jitsu patterns** — determine which pattern is dominant5. **Cross-reference Centre, Root and Firmament** — validate axis coherence6. **Document the conflict** — conflicts must be explicitly noted in the Integrated Analysis

### Examples of Edge Cases| Scenario | Resolution | BTL Consideration ||----------|------------|-------------------|| Structural: Upper-dominant; Energetic: Lower-dominant | Prioritise energetic data; note structural tendency | Check Posterior BTL tension vs. Anterior BTL shortness || Blockage in Spine + Excess in Shoulders | "Compensatory effort in upper body due to central tension" | Note Posterior BTL upper tension with Anterior BTL upper activation || Missing data for a region | Omit the region; do not speculate | Omit BTL description for unevaluated region || Jitsu in upper + Kyo in lower | Prioritize upper softening first | Anterior BTL upper + Anterior BTL lower different Flow States |
