## 1. Purpose of the Integrated Analysis

The Integrated Analysis answers three core questions:

1. **How is the body organised structurally?**  
2. **How is the body organised energetically?**  
3. **How do these two organisations interact?**

The output is a unified interpretation that describes:
- axis behaviour  
- compensatory logic  
- global tendencies  
- dominant patterns  
- functional consequences  

This interpretation is used to generate:
- therapeutic priorities  
- session sequencing  
- narrative tone  
- safety considerations  
- BTL (Base Therapeutic Load) adjustments

## 2. Structural–Energetic Fusion Model

The KISEI‑HŌ method does not treat structure and energy as separate domains.  
Instead, they form a **dual‑layer system**:

### Structural Layer  
- deviations  
- amplitudes  
- directional tendencies  
- segment behaviour  
- mechanical compensation  

### Energetic Layer  
- Ki flow  
- blockages  
- excess / deficiency  
- axis tone  
- global energetic patterns  

### Fusion Principle  
Structural behaviour expresses **mechanical adaptation**.  
Energetic behaviour expresses **functional adaptation**.

The Integrated Analysis merges both to reveal the **true organisational pattern**.

## 3. Axis Interpretation (Root → Centre → Firmament)

Axis behaviour is the backbone of the Integrated Analysis.

### Root  
Grounding, stability, lower-body organisation.

### Centre (Hara)  
Internal pressure, emotional tone, respiratory expansion.

### Firmament  
Upper-body expansion, cervical freedom, sensory orientation.

Axis interpretation determines:
- global tendencies  
- compensatory direction  
- energetic tone  
- structural coherence  

---

## 4. Pattern Classification

The Integrated Analysis identifies three types of patterns:

### 4.1. Structural-Dominant Patterns  
Structure dictates energetic behaviour.  
Example:  
Pelvic torsion → thoracic compensation → cervical rigidity.

### 4.2. Energetic-Dominant Patterns  
Energetic tone dictates structural behaviour.  
Example:  
Hara deficiency → collapse of thoracic expansion → forward head posture.

### 4.3. Mixed Patterns  
Most real cases.  
Example:  
Root Kyo + Centre Jitsu + Firmament Kyo  
→ unstable base, overactive core, weak projection.

Mixed patterns require cross-referencing with the Structural & Energetic Tables.

## 5. Conflict Resolution Rules

When structural and energetic readings diverge:

1. **Prioritise energetic data**  
Energetic states reflect the body’s current adaptive response.

2. **Validate axis behaviour**  
Root → Centre → Firmament must be coherent.

3. **Cross-reference structural deviations**  
Check if structural deviations support or contradict energetic tone.

4. **Consult global patterns**  
Use the Energetic Framework to identify Kyo/Jitsu tendencies.

5. **Document the conflict**  
Conflicts must be explicitly noted in the Integrated Analysis.

6. **Never speculate missing data**  
If a region is not evaluated, omit it.
