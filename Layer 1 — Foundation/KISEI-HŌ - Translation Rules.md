## 1. Translation Pipeline Overview

Translation follows **four mandatory stages** with **eight steps**:

### Stage 1: Technical Input → Classification
- **Step 1:** Technical Input Processing
- **Step 2:** Structural Classification
- **Step 3:** Energetic Classification

### Stage 2: Classification → Meaning
- **Step 4:** Meaning Assignment
- **Step 5:** Kyo/Jitsu Dynamic Integration

### Stage 3: Meaning → Editorial Narrative
- **Step 6:** Editorial Transformation
- **Step 7:** Persona Compliance Check

### Stage 4: Output Generation
- **Step 8:** Language-Appropriate Output

**No stage may be skipped. No step may be reordered.**

---

## 2. Stage 1 — Technical Input → Classification

### Step 1: Technical Input Processing

Technical input includes:
- structural deviations (effort, protection, compensation, adaptation)
- energetic states (flow, blockage, excess, deficiency)
- axis behaviour (Centre, Root, Firmament)
- global tendencies (upper/lower-dominant, anterior/posterior)
- Body Tension Line findings (BTL pathways)
- Energetic Flow State assessments
- Kyo/Jitsu dynamic patterns

**Processing rules:**
- Preserve all original technical data
- Identify all structural and energetic components
- Flag Body Tension Line mentions
- Flag Kyo/Jitsu pattern mentions
- Flag Energetic Flow State mentions

---

### Step 2: Structural Classification

Classification must follow:
- Structural & Energetic Tables
- PHYSIOCODE Clinical Architecture

**Structural classification categories:**
- Effort (increased work in a region)
- Protection (adaptive guarding)
- Compensation (one region supporting another)
- Adaptation (functional reorganization)
- Redistribution (shifting of load)
- Upper-Dominant Organization
- Lower-Dominant Organization
- Body Tension Line Pathway (BTL involvement)

**Classification rules:**
- Classification must remain neutral and descriptive
- Avoid diagnostic language
- Flag any Body Tension Line involvement
- Identify Kyo/Jitsu patterns if present

---

### Step 3: Energetic Classification

Classification must follow:
- Energetic Framework
- Glossary v1.1 (symbols, Flow States)

**Energetic classification categories:**
- Normal Flow → (Right Arrow)
- Blockage ⨂ (Crossed Circle)
- Excess ◎ (Bullseye)
- Deficiency ⊖ (Circle with Line)

**Energetic Flow States:**
- Smooth Flow (unobstructed movement)
- Stagnant Flow (partial obstruction)
- Rebellious Flow (counter-flow patterns)
- Deficient Flow (insufficient movement)
- Excess Flow (congested movement)

**Kyo/Jitsu patterns:**
- Kyo (虚): Deficiency — empty, weak, insufficient
- Jitsu (実): Excess — full, tense, congested

**Classification rules:**
- Use symbol notation where appropriate
- Describe Flow State in accessible terms
- Integrate Kyo/Jitsu pattern if identified

---

## 3. Stage 2 — Classification → Meaning

### Step 4: Meaning Assignment

**Meaning must:**
- describe adaptation (how the body responds)
- describe organisation (how the body structures effort)
- describe tendencies (patterns of behaviour)
- describe relationships (connections between regions)
- describe global behaviour (system-wide patterns)
- describe Body Tension Line significance (physical pathway implications)
- describe Flow State quality (energetic movement characteristics)
- describe Kyo/Jitsu dynamic (deficiency/excess relationship)

**Meaning must avoid:**
- pathology
- dysfunction
- correction
- emotional interpretation
- spiritual interpretation
- medical diagnosis
- performance framing

---

### Step 5: Kyo/Jitsu Dynamic Integration

When Kyo/Jitsu patterns are present, integrate them into meaning:

**For Kyo (deficiency) patterns:**
- Describe tissue/organ/channel as "less active," "showing reduced function"
- Translate to: "may respond gently to support," "could benefit from gradual activation"
- Connect to energy flow: "energy may move slowly through this area"

**For Jitsu (excess) patterns:**
- Describe tissue/organ/channel as "more active," "showing congestion"
- Translate to: "may respond to softening," "could benefit from gentle release"
- Connect to energy flow: "energy may be held or stuck in this area"

**For combined patterns:**
- Identify which pattern is dominant (Jitsu on surface, Kyo at root, or vice versa)
- Describe relationship: "congestion in upper regions with depletion in lower regions"
- Assign priority: "upper body requires softening first; lower body will benefit from gradual support"

---

## 4. Stage 3 — Classification → Meaning

### Step 6: Editorial Transformation

Editorial narrative must:
- follow the Narrative & Persona Guidelines v1.1
- remain calm, sensory and descriptive
- avoid lists and numbers
- avoid technical, clinical or biomechanical language
- avoid prescriptive or corrective language
- use humanized language
- maintain sensory presence ("you may notice," "your body might feel")
- integrate BTL descriptions in accessible terms
- describe Flow States in narrative form
- apply conditional framing ("may," "might," "could")

---

### Step 7: Persona Compliance Check

**Checklist before finalizing:**

1. **Tone:** Is it calm, slow, grounded?
2. **Language:** Is it human and accessible?
3. **Forbidden Terms:** Are any prohibited terms present? (Check Narrative & Persona Guidelines §12.1)
4. **Clinical Terms:** Have all been transformed from anamnesis data?
5. **BTL Descriptions:** Are Body Tension Lines described accessibly?
6. **Flow States:** Are Energetic Flow States described in narrative form?
7. **Kyo/Jitsu:** Are patterns described without clinical framing?
8. **Conditionality:** Is all language non-directive?
9. **Sensory Presence:** Is bodily experience described?

**If any check fails, return to Step 6 and revise.**

---

## 5. Stage 4 — Output Generation

### Step 8: Language-Appropriate Output

**For client outputs:**
- Translate to PT-PT or EN based on anamnesis language preference
- Use Narrative & Persona Guidelines for tone in target language
- Apply PT-PT conventions if client language is PT-PT
- Apply EN conventions if client language is EN

**For therapist outputs:**
- Generate in PT-PT with full technical detail
- Include symbol notation where appropriate
- Reference BTL and Flow State findings
- Include Kyo/Jitsu pattern analysis
- Maintain professional tone while following Narrative Guidelines

## 7. Full Translation Examples

### Example 1: Shoulder Elevation with BTL Involvement

**Anamnesis Input:**
"Herniated disc C5-C6, left shoulder elevation 12°, tight anterior chain"

**Steps 1-3: Classification**
- Structural: Upper region deviation (Compensation)
- BTL: Anterior Body Tension Line involved
- Energetic: Mild stagnation in upper region
- Kyo/Jitsu: Jitsu pattern (excess) in anterior chain

**Step 4-5: Meaning**
- The body compensates for cervical patterns by redistributing effort to upper region
- Anterior BTL shows tension from jaw through chest to hip flexors
- Energy held in upper body; lower body shows reduced activity (Kyo)
- Jitsu dominates in upper anterior; Kyo in lower regions

**Step 6-7: Editorial Narrative (PT-PT)**
"O seu corpo actualmente distribui o esforço de forma mais intensa na região superior, o que pode reflectir um padrão de protecção no pescoço e ombros. A linha anterior de tensão corporal mostra um acumular de esforço que vai do maxilar até aos flexores da anca — isto sugere que a energia pode estar a ser retida nesta via. Nas regiões inferiores, pode notar uma menor actividade, o que indica um padrão de menor presença energética. Ao trabalharmos a suavização na região superior, as regiões inferiores podem gradualmente encontrar maior equilíbrio."

**Step 8: Output (PT-PT — client language)**

### Example 2: Lower Back Blockage with Kyo Pattern

**Anamnesis Input:**
"L4-L5 disc bulge, chronic lower back pain, cold sensation in lower back"

**Steps 1-3: Classification**
- Structural: Lower region deviation (Effort, Protection)
- Energetic: Blockage (⨂) in core region
- Kyo/Jitsu: Kyo pattern (deficiency) — cold, weak, depleted

**Step 4-5: Meaning**
- Lower back holds protective effort as compensation for historical patterns
- Core shows energetic stagnation with deficiency characteristics
- Cold sensation indicates Kyo — insufficient energy flow
- Body adapts by redistributing load upward

**Step 6-7: Editorial Narrative (EN)**
"Your body tends to organize effort in a protective way around your lower back, which may reflect patterns established over time. Your core region shows a holding pattern that could be limiting the natural flow of energy. You might notice that this area feels cooler or less responsive — this suggests lower energy activity in this region. As energy begins to move more smoothly, warmth may gradually return to this area."

**Step 8: Output (EN — client language)**

### Example 3: Upper-Dominant Organization with Flow State

**Anamnesis Input:**
"Thoracic hyperkyphosis, shallow breathing, anxiety history"

**Steps 1-3: Classification**
- Structural: Upper-dominant organization (Redistribution)
- BTL: Posterior Body Tension Line involvement
- Energetic: Rebellious Flow (counter-flow, especially in breathing)
- Kyo/Jitsu: Jitsu in upper chest (fullness/tension), Kyo in lower abdomen (emptiness)

**Step 4-5: Meaning**
- Body organizes effort predominantly in upper region
- Posterior BTL shows tension from skull through spine to heels
- Breathing pattern affects energy circulation — upper chest holds, lower abdomen empty
- Rebellious Flow: energy moves upward instead of downward in cycle
- Pattern: upper Jitsu with lower Kyo — prioritize chest softening

**Step 6-7: Editorial Narrative (PT-PT)**
"A organização do seu corpo favorece actualmente a região superior — o esforço concentra-se no peito e na parte superior das costas. A linha posterior de tensão corporal mostra um acumular que vai da cabeça até aos calcanhares, o que pode limitar a respiração. A forma como a energia se move parece seguir um padrão ascendente em vez de circular naturalmente. Na zona do peito, pode haver uma sensação de plenitude ou tensão; no abdómen inferior, uma sensação de vazio ou menor actividade. Ao suavizarmos a região superior, o abdómen pode começar a encontrar maior vitalidade."

**Step 8: Output (PT-PT — client language)**

## 8. Body Tension Line Transformation Rules

**BTL descriptions must be transformed for narrative outputs:**

| BTL Finding | Clinical Description | Narrative Transformation |
|-------------|---------------------|--------------------------|
| Anterior BTL | Tension from jaw through chest to hip flexors | "A line of tension runs from the jaw through the chest to the front of the hips" |
| Posterior BTL | Tension from skull through spine to heels | "Tension accumulates along the back, from the head down to the heels" |
| Lateral BTL | Tension from ear through side body to foot | "A side line of tension runs from the ear along the body to the foot" |
| Spiral BTL | Tension crossing diagonally across the body | "A crossing pattern of tension moves diagonally across the body" |

## 9. Energetic Flow State Transformation Rules

**Flow State descriptions must be transformed for narrative outputs:**

| Flow State | Clinical Description | Narrative Transformation |
|------------|---------------------|--------------------------|
| Smooth Flow → | Unobstructed energy movement | "Energy moves smoothly through the channels" |
| Stagnant Flow ⨂ | Partial or complete obstruction | "Energy may be held or moving slowly in this area" |
| Rebellious Flow | Counter-flow or reverse movement | "Energy seems to move against its natural direction" |
| Deficient Flow ⊖ | Insufficient energy movement | "Energy movement may be reduced or weak in this area" |
| Excess Flow ◎ | Congested or overactive movement | "Energy may feel held or accumulated in this area" |
