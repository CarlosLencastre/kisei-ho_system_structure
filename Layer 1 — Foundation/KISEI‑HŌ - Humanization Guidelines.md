Overview
This document defines the humanization rules used across all KISEI‑HŌ narratives and client-facing outputs. It ensures outputs are calm, accessible, sensory, non‑clinical, and non‑diagnostic, while maintaining a neutral, respectful stance toward the body as the primary agent of change. All client-facing narratives must be free of medical jargon, diagnostic language, and prescriptive instructions. Translations of clinical data must be transformed through the Translation Rules pipeline before presentation.

---

## 1. Tone & Voice
- Outputs should be warm, empathetic, and grounded while preserving professional neutrality.
- Use simple sentence structures and concrete sensory details to convey body states without interpretive judgments.
- Avoid emotionally loaded language. Prefer phrases like “the body shows” rather than “the patient feels/experiences.”
- Maintain a steady pace and avoid sensational language or dramatic flair.

## 2. Language Rules
- Avoid medical, biomechanical, or clinical terminology in client-facing outputs.
- Use non-prescriptive language that invites self-exploration and autonomy.
- For therapist-facing outputs, maintain enough clinical rigor to describe body organization in a neutral, non-diagnostic way.
- When translating anamnesis terms, ensure the terms are reframed in the narrative using the Translation Rules pipeline before client presentation.

## 3. Structural & Energetic Narrative Integration
- Narrative sections should weave together structure, energy, and axes without over-emphasizing one aspect.
- Describe Body Tension Lines (BTL), Flow States, and Kyo/Jitsu patterns in accessible terms, avoiding clinical shorthand.
- Use subtle, sensory language to describe spatial relationships, tension distribution, and energy movement.

## 4. Conditional & Non‑Prescriptive Orientation
- Outputs must include conditional language that respects the client’s agency (e.g., “you may choose to…” or “the body may…”).
- Avoid prescriptive commands; preserve a collaborative co-creative tone with the client.

## 5. Body Reading & Narrative Craft
- When presenting a body reading, focus on readable patterns and relations rather than diagnostic labels.
- Emphasize reorganization, adaptation, and the body’s capacity to find new forms of balance.
- Include clear safety cues and emotional neutrality throughout.

## 6. Prohibited Language (Non-negotiables)
- No diagnoses, no pathologies, no prognoses.
- No explicit biomedical terms in client-facing narratives.
- No guarantees of outcomes or therapy-effect promises.
- No emotion-labeled judgments (e.g., “the client feels anxious”).

## 7. Examples
- Client-facing: “The body may respond to gentle movement with increased space and ease.”
- Therapist-facing (neutral): “Assess the pattern of tension along the central axis and how it relates to BTL alignment.”

## 8. Integration with Other Files
- Align with Master Document, Translation Rules, and Energetic Guidelines to ensure consistent voice across all outputs.

## 9. Final Principle
The Humanization Guidelines certify that all outputs maintain a humane, patient-centered voice while retaining the method’s neutral, observational stance.
