## 1. Purpose of the Therapeutic Plan

The Therapeutic Plan exists to:
- reflect the body's current organisation (structural, BTL, energetic, Kyo/Jitsu)
- describe structural, BTL, and energetic tendencies (in accessible language)
- outline a possible therapeutic direction (conditional, non-prescriptive)
- support gradual reorganisation (gentle, non-corrective)
- reinforce autonomy (client choice and pace)
- **describe BTL patterns accessibly** (via Translation Rules)
- **describe Flow States in narrative form** (via Translation Rules)
- **describe Kyo/Jitsu patterns without clinical terminology** (via Translation Rules)

It is descriptive, not prescriptive.

---

## 2. Structure of the Therapeutic Plan (v1.1 Updated)

The Therapeutic Plan must include:
- recap of the body's organisation (structural, BTL, Flow States, Kyo/Jitsu)
- structural tendencies (in accessible language)
- BTL involvement (primary pathway, tension level)
- energetic tendencies (with Flow States, in narrative form)
- Kyo/Jitsu balance (deficiency/excess relationship, in accessible language)
- axis behaviour (Centre, Root, Firmament)
- integrated pattern (combined synthesis)
- therapeutic direction (conditional, BTL/Kyo-Jitsu aligned)

---

## 3. Language Rules (v1.1 Updated)

The Therapeutic Plan must:
- use calm, grounded, and sensory language
- avoid clinical, biomechanical, or prescriptive terms in outputs
- remain body-centred and descriptive
- use conditional language (e.g., "It may be helpful to...", "Your body may explore...")
- avoid emotional interpretation
- avoid spiritual or esoteric language
- avoid prescriptive or corrective language
- **describe BTL accessibly** (not technical BTL jargon)
- **describe Flow States in narrative form** (not Flow State terminology)
- **describe Kyo/Jitsu without clinical terminology** (not "Jitsu pattern," use "more active/fuller/warmer")
- follow Safety & Compliance v1.1 (no forbidden terms)
- follow language rules v1.1 (PT-PT therapist, PT-PT/EN client outputs)

---

## 4. Therapeutic Direction (v1.1 Updated)

Therapeutic direction must describe:
- structural intention (gentle, non-corrective)
- energetic intention (supporting natural flow)
- BTL-aligned movement qualities (softening, lengthening along BTL pathways)
- Flow State-aligned movement qualities (based on dominant Flow State)
- Kyo/Jitsu balancing movement qualities (softening Jitsu, supporting Kyo)
- possible session rhythm (gentle, progressive)
- Five Element movement correlations (if applicable)

All orientation must remain conditional and non-prescriptive.

## 5. Example Therapeutic Plan (v1.1 Updated)

### Client Output Example (PT-PT):

> O seu corpo actualmente distribui o esforço de forma mais intensa na região superior, com tensão ao longo das costas. Esta linha de tensão desde a cabeça até aos calcanhares pode estar a afectar a forma como a energia se move — a zona superior pode sentir-se mais activa ou com maior presença, enquanto a zona inferior pode ter menos vitalidade.
>
> Esta organização pode reflectir um padrão adaptativo onde o corpo mantém estabilidade na zona superior enquanto a zona inferior encontra menor apoio.
>
> Uma direcção terapêutica possível pode explorar movimentos que permitam suavizar a zona superior — especialmente ao longo das costas — enquanto a zona inferior pode beneficiar gradualmente de movimentos que apoiem a sua vitalidade. O ritmo das sessões pode ser gentil e progressivo, permitindo que o corpo encontre novas formas de organização de forma natural.

### Therapist Reference (PT-PT):

> **Resumo Técnico (internal use):**
> - BTL: Posterior dominant, marked tension
> - Flow State: Upper stagnant, lower deficient
> - Kyo/Jitsu: Upper Jitsu, lower Kyo
> - Therapeutic priority: Soften upper (Jitsu), support lower (Kyo)
> - Movement qualities: Softening along posterior BTL, grounding for lower body
> - Session rhythm: Gentle, conservative approach
