## 1. Purpose of the Integrated Analysis

The Integrated Analysis answers four core questions (updated to v1.1):

1. **How is the body organised structurally?**
2. **How is the body organised energetically?**
3. **How do Body Tension Lines (BTL) and Flow States reflect the body's pattern?**
4. **How do these organisations interact (including Kyo/Jitsu balance)?**

The output is a unified interpretation that describes:
- axis behaviour
- compensatory logic
- global tendencies
- dominant patterns
- functional consequences
- **BTL involvement and tension patterns**
- **Flow State distribution**
- **Kyo/Jitsu balance**

This interpretation is used to generate:
- therapeutic priorities
- session sequencing
- narrative tone
- safety considerations
- BTL (Base Therapeutic Load) adjustments
- **Kyo/Jitsu balancing priorities**
- **Flow State movement qualities**

## 2. Structural–Energetic Fusion Model

The KISEI-HŌ method does not treat structure and energy as separate domains.
Instead, they form a **dual-layer system**:

### Structural Layer
- deviations
- amplitudes
- directional tendencies
- segment behaviour
- mechanical compensation
- **BTL involvement and tension pathways**

### Energetic Layer
- Ki flow
- blockages
- excess/deficiency (Jitsu/Kyo)
- axis tone
- global energetic patterns
- **Flow States**
- **Kyo/Jitsu patterns**

### Fusion Principle
Structural behaviour expresses **mechanical adaptation**.
Energetic behaviour expresses **functional adaptation**.
**BTL reflects the physical pathway of energetic patterns.**
**Flow States describe the quality of energy movement.**
**Kyo/Jitsu patterns describe the balance of deficiency and excess.**

The Integrated Analysis merges all to reveal the **true organisational pattern**.

## 3. BTL Integration (v1.1 NEW)

Body Tension Lines provide the physical pathway framework for structural-energetic patterns.

### BTL Assessment Protocol

1. **Identify primary BTL involvement** (Anterior, Posterior, Lateral, or Spiral)
2. **Assess tension level** (Mild, Moderate, Marked)
3. **Map BTL junctions** (where BTL crosses joints or other BTLs)
4. **Correlate with Flow States** along the BTL
5. **Identify Kyo/Jitsu pattern** at key BTL locations
6. **Determine BTL contribution to global pattern**

### BTL Pattern Types

| Pattern | BTL Involvement | Structural Correlation | Energetic Correlation | Kyo/Jitsu Balance |
|---------|-----------------|----------------------|----------------------|-------------------|
| Upper-dominant | Posterior BTL dominant, Anterior BTL shortened | Chest expansion limited | Flow held in upper body | Often Jitsu upper |
| Lower-dominant | Anterior BTL dominant, Posterior BTL lengthened | Hip mobility restricted | Flow pushed to lower body | Often Kyo lower |
| Lateral collapse | Lateral BTL tension on one side | Side bending, leg length | Asymmetric flow | Often unilateral Jitsu or Kyo |
| Spiral deviation | Spiral BTL tension | Rotation limited | Diagonal disruption | Mixed Kyo-Jitsu |
| Global tension | Multiple BTLs involved | Widespread restriction | Global Flow disruption | Variable Kyo-Jitsu |

## 4. Flow State Integration (v1.1 NEW)

Flow States describe the quality and movement of energy through the body.

### Flow State Assessment Protocol

1. **Assess Flow State in each major region** (upper, middle, lower)
2. **Identify Flow State interactions** between regions
3. **Determine Flow State contribution to global pattern**
4. **Map Flow State changes along BTL pathways**
5. **Correlate Flow States with Kyo/Jitsu patterns**

### Flow State Pattern Types

| Pattern | Upper Flow State | Middle Flow State | Lower Flow State | Kyo/Jitsu Correlation |
|---------|-----------------|-------------------|------------------|----------------------|
| Upper-stagnant | Stagnant Flow (⨂) | Smooth Flow (→) | Deficient Flow (⊖) | Upper Jitsu, Lower Kyo |
| Rebellious-distribution | Rebellious Flow (⨂↔) | Stagnant Flow (⨂) | Deficient Flow (⊖) | Upper Jitsu, Lower Kyo |
| Excess-accumulation | Excess Flow (◎) | Excess Flow (◎) | Normal Flow (→) | Global Jitsu |
| Deficient-collapse | Deficient Flow (⊖) | Deficient Flow (⊖) | Deficient Flow (⊖) | Global Kyo |
| Mixed-states | Variable | Variable | Variable | Mixed Kyo-Jitsu |

## 5. Kyo/Jitsu Pattern Integration (v1.1 NEW)

Kyo/Jitsu patterns describe the balance between deficiency and excess.

### Kyo/Jitsu Assessment Protocol

1. **Identify Kyo (deficiency) regions**
2. **Identify Jitsu (excess) regions**
3. **Determine dominant pattern** (Kyo-dominant, Jitsu-dominant, or mixed)
4. **Identify pattern interactions** between regions
5. **Determine therapeutic priority** based on Kyo-Jitsu balance

### Kyo/Jitsu Pattern Types

| Pattern | Upper Region | Middle Region | Lower Region | Balance Type | Therapeutic Priority |
|---------|--------------|---------------|--------------|--------------|---------------------|
| Upper-excess, lower-deficiency | Jitsu | Neutral | Kyo | Jitsu-dominant | Soften upper first, then support lower |
| Upper-deficiency, lower-excess | Kyo | Neutral | Jitsu | Mixed | Support upper first, then soften lower |
| Central-excess, peripheral-deficiency | Kyo | Jitsu | Kyo | Jitsu-dominant | Soften centre first, support periphery |
| Global-deficiency | Kyo | Kyo | Kyo | Kyo-dominant | Nurture all regions |
| Global-excess | Jitsu | Jitsu | Jitsu | Jitsu-dominant | Soften all regions |
| Mixed-dominant | Variable | Variable | Variable | Variable | Prioritize acute pattern |

## 6. Combined Pattern Synthesis (v1.1 NEW)

**Integration Rule:** All pattern types (BTL, Flow States, Kyo/Jitsu) must be synthesized together.

### Synthesis Protocol

1. **Map BTL primary involvement** — which BTL dominates the pattern
2. **Assess Flow States** along the BTL and in major regions
3. **Identify Kyo/Jitsu balance** at key BTL locations
4. **Determine dominant pattern type** — structural, energetic, or mixed
5. **Establish therapeutic priority** based on combined pattern

### Combined Pattern Examples

**Example 1: Upper-dominant Jitsu pattern**
- **BTL:** Posterior BTL dominant, tension from skull to heels
- **Flow States:** Upper Excess Flow, Middle Stagnant Flow, Lower Deficient Flow
- **Kyo/Jitsu:** Upper Jitsu, Lower Kyo
- **Synthesis:** The body shows upper-dominant organization with posterior BTL tension. Energy is accumulated in the upper body (excess) while the lower body shows depletion (deficient). The upper region is tense and full (Jitsu) while the lower region is weak and empty (Kyo).
- **Therapeutic Priority:** Soften upper body (Jitsu dispersion), support lower body (Kyo tonification)

**Example 2: Lateral collapse pattern**
- **BTL:** Right Lateral BTL tension, Right Anterior BTL shortened
- **Flow States:** Right side Stagnant Flow, Left side Deficient Flow
- **Kyo/Jitsu:** Right side Jitsu, Left side Kyo
- **Synthesis:** The body shows lateral collapse to the right side. The right BTL shows tension while the left side shows depletion. Energy is held on the right (stagnant) and reduced on the left (deficient).
- **Therapeutic Priority:** Soften right side (Jitsu), support left side (Kyo), restore lateral balance

**Example 3: Global deficiency pattern**
- **BTL:** Multiple BTLs with reduced tension (no strong dominant pathway)
- **Flow States:** Global Deficient Flow
- **Kyo/Jitsu:** Global Kyo (all regions)
- **Synthesis:** The body shows global energetic depletion. BTL tension is reduced throughout, and energy movement is weak in all regions. All areas show deficiency rather than excess.
- **Therapeutic Priority:** Nurture all regions gently, build energy gradually

## 7. Axis Interpretation (Root → Centre → Firmament)

Axis behaviour is the backbone of the Integrated Analysis.

### Root
Grounding, stability, lower-body organisation.
Correlates with BTL (lower pathways) and Flow States (lower body).

### Centre (Hara)
Internal pressure, emotional tone, respiratory expansion.
Correlates with BTL (central pathways) and Flow States (middle body).

### Firmament
Upper-body expansion, cervical freedom, sensory orientation.
Correlates with BTL (upper pathways) and Flow States (upper body).

**Axis-BTL-Flow State-Kyo/Jitsu Correlation:**

| Axis | BTL Correlation | Flow State Indicator | Kyo/Jitsu Indicator |
|------|-----------------|---------------------|---------------------|
| Root | Posterior BTL lower, Anterior BTL lower | Deficient Flow in lower body | Kyo in lower regions |
| Centre | Central BTL pathways | Stagnant or Deficient Flow in middle | Mixed or Jitsu in centre |
| Firmament | Posterior BTL upper, Anterior BTL upper | Excess or Rebellious Flow in upper body | Jitsu in upper regions |

Axis interpretation determines:
- global tendencies
- compensatory direction
- energetic tone
- structural coherence
- BTL contribution
- Flow State distribution
- Kyo/Jitsu balance

## 8. Pattern Classification

The Integrated Analysis identifies pattern types:

### 8.1. Structural-Dominant Patterns
Structure dictates energetic behaviour.
Example:
Pelvic torsion → BTL distortion → thoracic compensation → cervical rigidity → upper Flow State disruption.

### 8.2. Energetic-Dominant Patterns
Energetic tone dictates structural behaviour.
Example:
Hara Kyo with Deficient Flow → collapse of thoracic expansion → forward head posture → Posterior BTL tension.

### 8.3. BTL-Dominant Patterns (v1.1 NEW)
BTL tension pathways drive both structural and energetic patterns.
Example:
Anterior BTL dominant → chest held → energy stagnation in upper body → Jitsu in upper regions.

### 8.4. Mixed Patterns
Most real cases.
Example:
Root Kyo + Centre Jitsu + Firmament Kyo with Posterior BTL dominant
→ unstable base, overactive core, weak projection, posterior tension.

Mixed patterns require cross-referencing with the Structural & Energetic Tables v1.1.

## 9. Conflict Resolution Rules

When structural, energetic, BTL, Flow State, or Kyo/Jitsu data diverge:

1. **Prioritise energetic data** — energetic states reflect the body's current adaptive response
2. **Validate BTL findings** — check if BTL tension correlates with other data
3. **Assess Flow States** — Flow States provide quality beyond simple state
4. **Identify Kyo/Jitsu balance** — determine which pattern is dominant
5. **Validate axis behaviour** — Root → Centre → Firmament must be coherent
6. **Cross-reference structural deviations** — check if deviations support or contradict energetic tone
7. **Consult global patterns** — use Energetic Framework to identify tendencies
8. **Document the conflict** — conflicts must be explicitly noted in the Integrated Analysis
9. **Never speculate missing data** — if a region is not evaluated, omit it

## 14. Safety Integration

Safety rules must be applied when:
- energetic excess (Jitsu) affects cervical region
- energetic deficiency (Kyo) affects grounding
- Flow State shows Rebellious Flow in upper body
- Flow State shows Excess Flow with marked BTL tension
- Flow State shows Deficient Flow with global Kyo
- thoracic rigidity affects breathing with Stagnant Flow
- diagonal blockage affects mobility with Spiral BTL
- structural deviations exceed marked amplitude with Jitsu pattern

Safety flags must be included in the Integrated Analysis, including Flow State and Kyo/Jitsu considerations.
