## 1. Purpose

The Unified Prescription Rules ensure that all movement-based orientation follows:

- structural logic
- energetic coherence (including Flow States)
- narrative neutrality
- conditional framing
- safety rules
- non-corrective principles
- consistency with the Integrated Exercise Library
- **BTL-aligned movement qualities**
- **Kyo/Jitsu balancing movement qualities**

Movement suggestions must support the body's adaptive pattern without imposing correction, performance or alignment.

---

## 2. Principles of Movement Orientation

### Conditional, never prescriptive
Use conditional framing:
"**If you choose to continue, your body may explore…**"

### Body-centred, not performance-centred
Movement supports **organisation**, not achievement.

### Energetically coherent
Movement qualities must reflect the dominant energetic state including Flow State and Kyo/Jitsu balance.

### Structurally relevant
Movement qualities must reflect the dominant structural pattern and BTL involvement.

### Gentle and progressive
No intensity, no repetitions, no sets, no performance cues.

### Non-corrective
Movement does not "fix posture"; it supports natural reorganisation.

### Non-medical
No clinical claims, rehabilitation framing or biomechanical terminology.

## 3. Structural Logic for Movement Selection

Movement qualities must follow the dominant structural pattern identified in the PHYSIOCODE analysis.

| Structural Pattern | Recommended Movement Categories | Example Narrative |
|--------------------|----------------------------------|-------------------|
| Upper-dominant organisation | Softening, lengthening, gentle expansion | "Your body may explore movements that soften the upper region." |
| Lower-dominant organisation | Grounding, centring, stabilising | "Your body may explore movements that support grounding in the lower body." |
| Balanced organisation | Gentle mobility, breathing-based, slow rhythmic movement | "Your body may explore movements that maintain its natural balance." |
| Protective patterns (effort/protection) | Softening, gentle compression | "Your body may explore softening movements where tension is held." |
| Compensatory patterns (compensation/adaptation) | Stabilising, lengthening | "Your body may explore stabilising movements to support redistribution." |

### Selection Rules

- If mixed patterns exist, prioritise the **most restrictive or dominant region**.
- If structural data is incomplete, default to **Gentle mobility** and flag for therapist review.
- Always cross-reference with the **Energetic Framework** to avoid mismatches.

| Energetic State | Movement Quality | Example Narrative |
|------------------|------------------|-------------------|

## 6. Kyo/Jitsu Balancing Movement Selection (v1.1 NEW)

Kyo/Jitsu patterns describe the balance between deficiency and excess, guiding movement qualities.

### Kyo (Deficiency) Movement Principles

| Kyo Level | Movement Quality | Example Narrative |
|-----------|------------------|-------------------|
| Mild Kyo | Gentle support, subtle activation | "Your body may explore gentle, supportive movement in this area." |
| Moderate Kyo | Nurturing movement, breathing support | "Your body may explore nurturing movement, allowing gentle breath to support this area." |
| Marked Kyo | Very gentle support, gradual building | "Your body may explore very gentle movement, supporting gradually without demand." |

### Jitsu (Excess) Movement Principles

| Jitsu Level | Movement Quality | Example Narrative |
|-------------|------------------|-------------------|
| Mild Jitsu | Softening, allowing release | "Your body may explore softening movement, allowing release." |
| Moderate Jitsu | Gentle release, breath-supported softening | "Your body may explore gentle release, allowing breath to support softening." |
| Marked Jitsu | Slow, deliberate softening, minimal intervention | "Your body may explore slow, deliberate softening, allowing the area to release naturally." |

### Mixed Kyo-Jitsu Movement Principles

| Pattern | Movement Strategy | Example Narrative |
|---------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Upper Jitsu + Lower Kyo | Soften upper first, then support lower | "Your body may explore softening in the upper region, then gentle support toward the lower body." |
| Upper Kyo + Lower Jitsu | Support upper first, then soften lower | "Your body may explore gentle support in the upper region, then softening toward the lower body." |
| Central Jitsu + Peripheral Kyo | Soften centre first, support periphery | "Your body may explore softening in the central area, then gentle support toward the periphery." |
| Global Kyo | Nurture all regions gently | "Your body may explore gentle, nurturing movement throughout, supporting without demand." |
| Global Jitsu | Soften all regions slowly | "Your body may explore slow, softening movement throughout, allowing release." |

**Rules:**
- Kyo/Jitsu balance takes priority over Flow State in movement selection
- Prioritize softening Jitsu areas before supporting Kyo areas (unless Kyo is acute)
- Marked patterns require very gentle intervention

## 7. Combined Movement Selection (v1.1 NEW)

Movement selection must integrate all pattern types: structural, BTL, Flow State, and Kyo/Jitsu.

### Selection Priority

1. **Kyo/Jitsu balance** — determines general movement strategy
2. **Flow State** — modifies specific movement quality
3. **BTL involvement** — identifies physical pathway focus
4. **Structural pattern** — determines movement category

### Example: Combined Selection

**Pattern:** Upper-dominant, Posterior BTL, Upper Stagnant Flow, Upper Jitsu + Lower Kyo

**Selection:**
1. Kyo/Jitsu: Upper Jitsu + Lower Kyo → Soften upper, support lower
2. Flow State: Upper Stagnant → Softening movement with breath support
3. BTL: Posterior dominant → Lengthen along back, gentle spinal awareness
4. Structural: Upper-dominant → Softening upper region

**Combined Narrative:** "Your body may explore softening in the upper region along the back, allowing breath to support gentle release. Then, towards the lower body, gentle support may help this area find greater ease."

## 9. Conditional Narrative Rules

Movement orientation must follow the Narrative & Persona Guidelines v1.1:

### Allowed
- "You may notice…"
- "Your body might explore…"
- "If you choose to continue…"
- "It may be helpful to…"
- "Energy may move…"
- "Tension may soften…"
- "The body may find…"

### Prohibited
- "Do this."
- "You should."
- "Try to…"
- "Correct…"
- "Fix…"
- "Align…"
- "Strengthen…"
- "Stretch…"

All movement orientation must be **non-directive** and **non-performative**.

## 10. Safety Rules for Movement Orientation

Movement suggestions must respect safety flags identified in the Integrated Analysis:

- Cervical excess (Jitsu) → avoid upward expansion
- Thoracic rigidity with Stagnant Flow → avoid intensity or breath-holding
- Energetic deficiency (Kyo) → avoid demanding movements
- Diagonal collapse with Spiral BTL → avoid complex coordination
- Marked structural deviations with Jitsu → avoid load or range extremes
- Rebellious Flow in upper body → avoid rapid movement, use slow deliberate quality
- Deficient Flow globally → avoid any intensity, use very gentle nurturing
- Excess Flow with marked BTL tension → avoid active doing, use allowing quality

Safety overrides all structural, BTL, Flow State, and Kyo/Jitsu priorities.
