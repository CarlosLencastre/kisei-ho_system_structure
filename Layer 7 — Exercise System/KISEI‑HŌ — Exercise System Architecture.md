## 3. Movement Categories (v1.1 Updated)

The Exercise Plan uses the following categories from the Integrated Exercise Library:

### Structural Categories
- Grounding movements
- Centring movements
- Softening movements
- Stabilising movements
- Lengthening movements
- Breathing-based movements
- Gentle mobility
- Gentle expansion
- Gentle compression
- Slow rhythmic movement

### BTL-Aligned Categories (v1.1 NEW)
- **Anterior BTL movements** — softening front body, breath expansion through chest
- **Posterior BTL movements** — lengthening along back, gentle spinal awareness
- **Lateral BTL movements** — balance between sides, side body lengthening
- **Spiral BTL movements** — gentle rotation, finding easy turning

### Flow State Categories (v1.1 NEW)
- **Smooth Flow movements** — slow rhythmic movement, maintaining current state
- **Stagnant Flow movements** — softening, gentle expansion, breathing support
- **Rebellious Flow movements** — slow, deliberate movement, finding natural direction
- **Deficient Flow movements** — gentle, nurturing movement, supporting without forcing
- **Excess Flow movements** — softening movement, allowing rather than doing

### Kyo/Jitsu Balancing Categories (v1.1 NEW)
- **Jitsu-softening movements** — gentle release, breath-supported softening
- **Kyo-nurturing movements** — gentle support, breathing-based activation
- **Mixed Kyo-Jitsu movements** — balance approach (soften upper then support lower, or vice versa)

## 4. Principles (v1.1 Updated)

The Exercise Plan must:
- Be non-prescriptive and non-corrective
- Align with the client's structural pattern
- Align with the client's BTL involvement (primary pathway, tension level)
- Align with the client's Flow State distribution
- Align with the client's Kyo/Jitsu balance
- Use conditional language (e.g., "It may be helpful to explore...", "Your body may notice...")
- Avoid techniques, repetitions, sets, or performance framing
- Follow Sōtai principles (natural movement toward comfort, gentle correction without force)
- **describe BTL findings accessibly** (not technical BTL jargon)
- **describe Flow States in narrative form** (not Flow State terminology)
- **describe Kyo/Jitsu without clinical terminology** (not "Jitsu pattern," use "more active/less active")
- follow Safety & Compliance v1.1 (no forbidden terms)
- follow language rules v1.1 (PT-PT therapist, PT-PT/EN client outputs)

## 5. Movement Selection Logic (v1.1 Updated)

For detailed rules on how to select movement categories based on:
- Structural patterns (upper-dominant, lower-dominant, balanced, protective, compensatory)
- BTL involvement (Anterior, Posterior, Lateral, Spiral)
- Flow State distribution (Smooth, Stagnant, Rebellious, Deficient, Excess)
- Kyo/Jitsu balance (Jitsu-dominant, Kyo-dominant, Mixed)

Refer to `KISEI-HŌ - Unified Prescription Rules.md` (Section 3-7).
The selection must always respect:
- The client's Flow State as defined in the Energetic Framework v1.1
- The client's Kyo/Jitsu balance as defined in the Structural & Energetic Tables v1.1
- The client's BTL involvement as defined in the Structural & Energetic Tables v1.1
- Safety flags identified in the Integrated Analysis v1.1

Movement selection follows this priority:
1. Kyo/Jitsu balance — determines general movement strategy
2. Flow State — modifies specific movement quality
3. BTL involvement — identifies physical pathway focus
4. Structural pattern — determines movement category

## 6. Example Exercise Plan (v1.1 Updated)

### Client Output Example (PT-PT):

> **O seu corpo e os movimentos**
>
> Com base na forma como o seu corpo está organizado, podem ser explorados movimentos suaves que apoiem a sua natural reorganização.
>
> **Para a zona superior (onde a energia pode estar mais acumulada):**
> Podem ser explorados movimentos suaves ao longo das costas — permitindo que a coluna encontre apoio sem força. Na região do peito, movimentos que permitam suavizar podem ajudar a energia a fluir mais naturalmente.
>
> **Para a zona inferior (onde a energia pode ter menos vitalidade):**
> Movimentos gentis que apoiem sem exigir podem ajudar o corpo a encontrar maior vitalidade. Movimentos de enraizamento suave podem trazer maior estabilidade.
>
> **Qualidade geral dos movimentos:**
> Suave, lento, sem repetições ou esforço. O corpo pode encontrar o seu próprio ritmo. Se algo não se sentir confortável, pode simplesmente parar ou ajustar.
>
> Estes movimentos pretendem alinhar-se com a forma como o seu corpo está organizado — permitindo que encontre equilíbrio de forma natural.

### Therapist Reference (PT-PT):

> **Resumo Técnico (internal use):**
> - Structural pattern: Upper-dominant organisation
> - BTL: Posterior dominant, marked tension, junction at cervical and lumbar
> - Flow State: Upper stagnant, lower deficient
> - Kyo/Jitsu: Upper Jitsu, lower Kyo
> - Movement selection: Posterior BTL lengthening (upper), grounding movements (lower), softening upper first, then gentle support lower
> - Movement categories: Lengthening, softening, grounding, breathing-based
> - Sōtai principles: Natural movement toward comfort
